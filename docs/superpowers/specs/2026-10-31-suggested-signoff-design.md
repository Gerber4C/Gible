# Suggested Signoff Semantics

## Problem

Gible currently treats many calls tagged `signoff` as irrevocable terminal
placements.  In particular, its purpose axiom creates an automatic Pass when
the preceding contract also has `final_placement` and
`purpose_completed=True`.  This is wrong for a bridge *signoff suggestion*:
the caller has declined to cooperate, but an exceptionally strong partner may
still continue.

The verified Daqiush 1NT route is
`1NT–2C–2M–3oM–3NT–4x–4M`, where 4M follows a shown shortage. The final 4M
is currently marked `purpose_completed=True`; the purpose axiom therefore
pre-empts the responder's next decision with Pass.

An earlier audit suspicion about `1NT–2C–2M–4D–4M` was rejected. That
no-shortage refusal already omits `purpose_completed`, and its authored Pass
and 4NT continuations remain in scope for their own tests but are not changed
by this specification.

## Terminology and contract

`signoff` is the English label for a bid that recommends stopping at the
current contract.  It is not, by itself, an irrevocable end to the auction.

There are two distinct kinds of placement:

| Kind | Required metadata | Effect on partner's next call |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suggested signoff | `signoff`; may also be a natural placement | Pass remains available, but authored or derived continuations remain eligible. |
| Final placement | `final_placement` and `purpose_completed=True` | The purpose axiom may synthesize the terminal Pass and suppress further choices. |

`final_placement` remains behavioral metadata: it participates in frame and
contract semantics as well as display. It is not, on its own, sufficient to
close a purpose. Only `purpose_completed=True` authorizes the
terminal-purpose shortcut.

## Scope

This change is deliberately limited to the verified shortage family and its
metadata. It does not reorder generic candidate selection or reclassify all
existing game placements, serious-3NT endings, Texas completions, no-shortage
refusals, or keycard placements. Those need separate bidding-rule review.

The one exception is the generic **post-RKCB placement** rule below. It is not
a suggested signoff: when an RKCB asker actually places the agreed trump at
the five, six, or seven level after an answer, that contract is a forced
signoff and partner must Pass.

### Shortage route

For every major/shortage combination generated by
`stayman_*_after_*_shortage_stop`:

- 4M (or Pass where the displayed shortage call is already 4M) is a suggested
  signoff, not a completed purpose;
- it retains `signoff` and may retain `final_placement`, but it omits
  `purpose_completed`;
- the responder has two explicit candidates: Pass accepts the suggested
  signoff, while 4NT asks ordinary RKCB for the agreed major;
- the existing signoff-override selector chooses between those two candidates
  from public-information simulation, using its deterministic high-strength
  fallback only if simulation is unavailable;
- the shown shortage is tagged as a slam/control probe solely so that this
  existing signoff-override mechanism can see the route;
- at this exact continuation point an ordinary 4NT suppresses the competing
  Voidwood candidate. A known short suit is not re-asked as Exclusion.

For the concrete screenshot hand, South holds four heart-keycards, the heart
queen, and the club void. After South's 4NT, either `5C` or `5D` by North is
treated as confirming that the partnership has the required keycards and
trump queen. South does not reject a slam merely because the ambiguous 1430
answer could mean zero/three or one/four: the missing club ace is irrelevant
opposite the known void. This is a route-specific ordinary-RKCB interpretation,
not a global redefinition of 1430.

### Post-RKCB final placement

A keycard-purpose placement must carry public, replayable terminal semantics.
This fixes the independent failure represented by:

`1NT–2S–3C–3NT–4D–4NT–5H–6C–Pass`

Here `4NT` is ordinary RKCB for clubs, `5H` is the answer, and the asker's
`6C` places the small slam. The partner's next call is forced Pass; it must
never be TD.

The purpose-axiom candidate produced after a keycard answer therefore marks a
placement in the agreed trump at level 5, 6, or 7 as:

- `action_type: keycard_final_placement`;
- `call_act_types: [signoff, final_placement]`; and
- `purpose_completed: true`.

The same meaning must be reconstructed by auction replay without access to
the asker's private memory. It may be derived only when the public state says
ordinary RKCB is answered, the bidder is the original asker, and the call
places the agreed trump. It must then mark that keycard context completed.

This inference excludes all live continuations: a queen ask, the 5S relay,
a 5NT/king continuation, a new-suit call, and any bid not in the agreed trump
remain nonterminal and retain their existing meanings.

## Engine behavior

1. `terminal_pass_candidate` continues to require both `final_placement` and
   `purpose_completed=True`; no selector-order change is made.
2. Call specs that mean only suggested signoff omit `purpose_completed`; they
   retain the `signoff` act so display, axioms, and policy vocabulary remain
   consistent.
3. Existing generated candidates retain their present precedence. A generic
   derived Pass remains a fallback only when no authored/generated candidate
   exists.
4. The shortage family authors its own Pass and normal 4NT at the responder's
   next turn. This makes a weak hand's pass explicit and supplies the two
   candidates required by `signoff_override`; it does not rely on a generic
   derived pass while the inherited game force is active.
5. The ordinary-RKCB continuation receives a route flag recording the shown
   void and the asker's known keycards/trump queen. Only the documented
   `5C`/`5D` response case may use that flag to treat the keycard requirement
   as complete.
6. A completed `keycard_slam` purpose projects a public
   `keycard_final_placement` meaning when it places the agreed trump at level
   5/6/7. Replay derives the identical meaning from public RKCB state, so the
   partner's terminal Pass works for interactive bidding and scanner replay.

## Non-goals

- Replacing all authored continuations with a universal slam policy.
- Changing the meaning of a true final placement, accepted/rejected grand-slam
  invitation, or a completed keycard sequence.
- Broadening the special `5C`/`5D` keycard interpretation to other routes.
- Treating a queen ask, relay, king inquiry, or any non-trump post-RKCB call
  as a final placement.
- Implementing the broader extreme-hand descriptive proxy in this change; that
  is specified separately in
  `2026-10-30-extreme-hand-description-proxy-design.md`.

## Tests

Focused tests must cover:

1. the screenshot auction and hands: North selects 4H with `signoff` but no
   `purpose_completed`; South's candidate pool contains exactly the explicit
   Pass and ordinary 4NT continuation (not Voidwood 6C), and the strong South
   hand selects 4NT;
2. the symmetric spade and other-shortage variants;
3. a weak responder fixture at the same post-signoff point selects explicit
   Pass, proving that the signoff is suggested rather than forcing;
4. after 4NT, concrete `5C` and `5D` answer fixtures verify the special
   keycard/trump-queen completion record;
5. genuine completed placements still synthesize a terminal Pass;
6. the concrete club-RKCB process in this section replays South's 6C as
   `keycard_final_placement` and North selects Pass, without TD;
7. queen-ask/relay/king-continuation fixtures remain nonterminal;
8. no unrelated 1NT invitation or RKCB test is broadened unnecessarily.

Run the smallest affected test selection first: the owner-route tests,
one-notrump invitation tests, and the terminal-purpose/derived-semantics tests.
