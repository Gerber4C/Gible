### Natural and low-minor invitations (bounded executable routes)

- Ordinary 3NT invitation ignores minor length and treats the hand as featureless:
  `1NT–2C–2D/2H/2S–2NT`. Opener Passes with 15 HCP or less and bids 3NT with
  16 or more in this bounded route. Four-card-major invitations are separate.
- A **good-suit minor invitation** seeks 3NT, not 5m. With six-plus cards and
  at least two of A/K/Q in that minor, responder transfers (`2S` to clubs,
  `2NT` to diamonds). Ordinary acceptance means responder can Pass 3m;
  superaccept means the invitation is accepted and responder bids 3NT.
  The good-suit test follows the supplied CBB baseline and is an initial
  executable criterion, not a newly asserted owner definition.
- A weak hand wanting to play 3m uses the same transfer. After a superaccept,
  it rebids 3m; after ordinary acceptance, it Passes.
- These terminal placements explicitly mark their purpose complete. The
  shared purpose axiom supplies partner's closing Pass after an uncontested
  placement, so each route does not repeat a Pass rule.
- A seven-plus-card minor with 6–7 HCP and provisional K–R minus HCP of at
  least 2.0 has a distinct shapely 5m invitation:
  transfer then 4m. Opener accepts at 5m only when adjusted control points
  are at least 14.5 (ordinary 15-point minimum less 0.5). The trump suit keeps
  raw HCP; every other partner suit publicly known to contain four or more

- Current numerical tuning for featureless invitations is 8–9 HCP:
  an eight with K–R <= 6.5 or 4–3–3–3 shape stops; other eights can invite.
  `K–R − HCP >= 1.0` for an exceptionally good nine bids direct 3NT.
  These relative-quality cutoffs are provisional. Raw HCP is never changed.
- The owner's later statement about changing trump Q/J values for a
  nine- or ten-card fit was explicitly withdrawn and is **not** implemented.

### Shape, fit, and description

- A shapely hand is worth more than the same honors in a flat hand. Keep full
  honor values in **each** of its four-card-or-longer suits; do not discount
  the second suit merely because the first was named. A larger known fit is
  stronger than a smaller fit. Fit evidence must come from the public auction,
  never from the partner's actual hidden hand.
- When fit and shape make a point cutoff unreliable, sample hands compatible
  with the public auction and compare the proposed contracts by their make
  rates. The 5-5-major spade-fit slam choice uses this route for all hands in
  the authored game-forcing class, not only those with 14 raw HCP.
- If a shapely hand has no precise descriptive call, the profile may use a
  *bidding description proxy*: shorten a 7+ long suit (or a 6+ suit with a
  second four-card suit) by one card and add two **descriptive** effective
  points. This changes only which convention is selected; it does not change
  the real cards, raw HCP, K–R, or simulation input. The 6m–5M hand's major
  invitation uses this proxy to enter the nearest authored route.

### Executable Jacoby superaccept slice

An exactly five-card major with at most three cards in the other major starts
`2D` for hearts or `2H` for spades. A six-card major hand meeting the approved
slam-interest cutoffs below also starts Jacoby. The transfer is a directive,
not an invitation/game/stop label: it does not publicly promise a particular
strength class. The strength axioms still apply to responder's later calls
when a corresponding meaning or termination is at issue.

With absolute-maximum strength and at least four-card support, opener shows a
doubleton. After a heart transfer, `2S` / `2NT` / `3C` shows a spade / diamond /
club doubleton; after a spade transfer, `2NT` / `3C` / `3D` shows a heart / club /
diamond doubleton. A four-card-major 4333 absolute maximum instead bids `3M`.
Other hands accept at `2M`. The existing fit-adjusted absolute-maximum test is
used; no new point threshold was invented here.

After any of those eight superaccepts, a responder already classified as
six-card-major slam-interest immediately bids `4NT`, agrees the transferred
major and asks 1430 keycards. Opener's four ordinary 1430 answers are available.
Five-card hands can start the transfer but their later strength-dependent
continuations remain deliberately unimplemented; no automatic `4NT` is inferred
from length alone. Interference is not covered by this slice.
